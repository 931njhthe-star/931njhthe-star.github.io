01 / SYSTEM OVERVIEW

From simulated sensors to measurable results.

A single workflow collects synchronized sensor data, runs detection and tracking, estimates inter-vehicle distance, and records results for replay and evaluation.

01 · INPUT

CARLA sensor streams

RGB camera, radar, IMU, GNSS, vehicle state, and event sensors.

02 · DETECT

YOLO11n inference

C++/CUDA TensorRT module returns vehicle detections to the runtime.

03 · TRACK

Camera + radar tracking

Vehicle IDs use class, IoU, and center distance, with radar-assisted association and position correction.

04 · ESTIMATE

Distance calculation

Camera ground projection with conditional radar distance correction.

05 · REVIEW

Monitor, record, evaluate

Qt dashboard, session recording, saved-input replay, and Ground Truth comparison.

02 / REAL-TIME MONITORING

03 / EVALUATION DESIGN

Separate radar distance correction from track correction.

Three methods run on the same recorded input. This keeps the comparison focused on the contribution of each step.

M1 · BASELINE

Image tracking + camera distance

YOLO detections are associated by class, IoU, and center distance. Distance is estimated by projecting the tracked box onto the ground plane.

M2 · DISTANCE FUSION

Camera tracking + conditional radar distance

Uses the M1 tracks. When radar cluster quality and consistency checks pass, the camera distance is replaced with radar distance; otherwise the valid camera estimate is retained.

M3 · RADAR-ASSISTED

Radar-assisted tracking + distance fusion

Adds radar-based association and position correction during camera detection gaps, then applies the same conditional distance rule used by M2.

The 437 radar-corrected samples are a subset of the 16,491 common samples. Their larger gains apply to this selected subset; they should not be interpreted as the overall system improvement.

Overall results and conditional gains tell different parts of the story.

Across all common samples, M3 reduced MAE by 5.41% and RMSE by 0.83% compared with M1. Among 437 predictions where radar corrected the track position, M3 reduced MAE by 39.42% and RMSE by 25.15%. The overall result remains modest because the corrected cases account for 2.65% of the common sample set.

05 / EXPERIMENTS & LIMITS

Controlled simulation, clearly scoped conclusions.

CARLA provides repeatable conditions and Ground Truth for quantitative analysis. The results describe simulation experiments, not proof of real-road performance.

Nine weather and time-of-day sessions

Data was collected for clear, cloudy, and heavy-rain conditions across noon, sunset, and night. Each condition was collected once for approximately two minutes, then evaluated with the same saved input.

Clear · NoonCloudy · SunsetHeavy Rain · Night

Interpretation and limitations

The experiment uses a limited set of CARLA scenarios. The 437 radar-corrected samples are only 2.65% of all common samples, and scene composition differs between weather/time sessions. The evaluation therefore does not isolate weather effects or establish generalization to real-road conditions.

Next steps include repeated runs with matched traffic and routes, broader distance and occlusion scenarios, and validation with real sensor data. Future evaluation can also include track continuity, ID switches, missed-detection recovery, and processing latency.
